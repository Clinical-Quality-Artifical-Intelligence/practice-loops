# NMC Assessment Methods & Supervision Guidance

Derived from *Proficiencies Tool 2020 (NMC 2018 Standards of Proficiency for Registered Nurses)*.

## 1. Five Valid Assessment Methods

The NMC specifies five methods to evidence completion of proficiencies:

| Assessment Method | Scope & Guidance | Example Proficiencies |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Observation** | Required when a skill must be physically demonstrated. The supervisor observes performance in real time. | Hand washing, vital signs, aseptic technique, physical procedures |
| **Demonstration through Discussion** | Used to assess clinical reasoning, theoretical knowledge, legal frameworks, or rationale behind care decisions. | Mental Capacity Act rationale, duty of candour, ethical dilemmas |
| **Simulation** | Experiential learning with repetition, feedback, evaluation, and reflection in lab/simulated environments. | Complex emergency response, rare procedures, NG tube insertion |
| **Spoke Placement / Training Opportunity** | Placements in specialist or alternative care environments to gain exposure unavailable in the primary area. | End-of-life care spoke, tissue viability spoke, community contintence spoke |
| **Feedback** | Input gathered from service users, carers, or multi-disciplinary team (MDT) members. | Therapeutic communication, person-centred care, dignity |

> **Note**: A single nursing intervention can demonstrate a range of proficiencies simultaneously (e.g. a discharge process demonstrates communication, risk assessment, care planning, and multi-agency working).

---

## 2. Key NMC Regulatory Definitions

### "Demonstrate"
- *Practical exhibition*: Showing how a skill, intervention, or process works in practice.
- *Proof or evidence*: Providing documentary proof (e.g. Practice Assessment Document, written reflection, care plan entry).

### "Practicable"
- Learning and assessment opportunities are circumstantial (dependent on patient presentation, local service provision, and setting).
- Flexible, outcome-focused approaches are encouraged within NMC education standards.

---

## 3. Invasive Clinical Skills & Supervision Rules

> [!WARNING]
> ### Safety-Critical Skills Rule
> Proficiencies marked with an asterisk (`*`) (e.g. self-harm risk assessment, end-of-life care planning, aseptic wound care, NG tube insertion) require **direct supervision continuously**, even after passing the proficiency on a previous placement.
> Passing a proficiency once does not assume continuous unsupervised competence for invasive or high-risk skills.
